Apocalypse Store – Sales Dashboard
Project overview
This Power BI project explores sales performance for a fictional prepping store business. It brings together sales, product and customer information to help users explore what is selling, how sales differ across customer groups, and how product performance can be compared.

The aim was not just to display figures, but to practise turning sales data into information that could support business questions and decisions.
Dashboard contents
The report contains two pages:
1. Sales Dashboard
The main page brings together key performance indicators and product/customer views, including:
- Total Revenue
- Total Units Sold
- Total Profit
- Profit Margin
- Top 3 best-selling products, ranked by units sold
- Revenue by age group
- Total sales by customer
- Products sold by product
- A product table showing product name, price and total units sold
Interactive filters allow the user to explore the data by:
- Month purchased
- Product name
- State
2. Customer Insight
A matrix-style view compares total units sold across product names and customer age groups. Conditional formatting helps make differences in sales volume easier to spot.
Business questions explored
The dashboard is designed to help explore questions such as:
- Which products sell the most units?
- How does revenue vary across customer age groups?
- How are sales distributed across customers?
- How do product prices and sales volumes compare?
- Does the picture change when filtering by month, product or state?
These views are starting points for analysis. They do not, on their own, explain why a product or customer group performs differently.
Key learning and analytical judgement
An important part of this project was learning to look beyond individual headline figures.

For example, a product with high sales volume or revenue is not automatically the most profitable product. Revenue, production costs, profit and profit margin need to be considered together before drawing conclusions.

The project also reinforced that:
- Choosing the right metric matters as much as choosing the visual.
- Filters help users investigate patterns from different perspectives.
- A visual should answer a business question, rather than simply fill dashboard space.
- Findings should be treated as observations until they are checked against the underlying data and business context.

Notes
- This is a learning and portfolio project based on a prepping store sales dataset.
- The dashboard supports exploratory analysis; it does not establish causes behind the sales patterns.
- Profit margin should be formatted as a percentage in Power BI. For example, a stored decimal value of 0.95 displays as 95% when percentage formatting is applied. The displayed result should still be checked against the underlying revenue and profit calculations.
